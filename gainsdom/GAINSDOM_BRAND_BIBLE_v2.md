### The Discipline Engine · Brand + Motion System

> **Stop waiting. Start small. Never miss twice.**

This file is the single source of truth for GainsDom. Anyone making content for the brand (a human editor or an AI agent) reads it fully before creating anything.

## 1. BRAND CORE

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | GainsDom |
| **System name** | The Discipline Engine |
| **One-line description** | A discipline system for people who keep quitting. |
| **Tagline** | Stop waiting. Start small. Never miss twice. |
| **Mission** | Turn relatable struggles — laziness, inconsistency, distraction, weak routines, low confidence, stalled progress — into clear missions, systems and actions. |
| **The enemy** | **Waiting.** Waiting for motivation, for Monday, for the right mood, for feeling ready. |
| **The promise** | Systems, not motivation — so you stop quitting. |
| **The format** | Faceless. Short-form vertical video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) + carousels. |

**The core belief:** People aren't lazy. They're stuck in a start-and-quit loop because they rely on motivation, and motivation is unreliable. A system works on the days you don't feel like it.

**The metaphor:** Motivation is weather — it changes, you can't control it. Discipline is an engine — you build it, you maintain it, it runs no matter the weather. All it needs is ignition: one small first action.

**The world (new in v2):** Every GainsDom video takes place inside **GAINSDOM/OS** — a dark, calm operating system that runs your discipline. Loading bars, installers, logs, error screens and protocols are not random decoration: they are the brand's world. The viewer is the **Operator**. This one world is what makes 100 videos feel like one brand.

## 2. AUDIENCE

**Primary:** Young people (roughly 16–25) who train at home, keep starting and quitting, and are tired of motivation that doesn't last.

**What they feel:**
- "I always start strong and fall off after 3 days."
- "I know what to do, I just don't do it."
- "I waste my nights on my phone and hate myself in the morning."
- "Everyone else seems so disciplined."

**What they want:** To finally be consistent. To feel in control. To become stronger and more capable — quietly, without needing to perform for anyone.

**How they watch (design for this):** On a phone, often with sound off, thumb ready to scroll. They decide in under 2 seconds. They rewatch short loops. They save what's useful and share what's relatable.

**Expansion later:** Students, focus/productivity, routines, confidence — everyone stuck in the start-and-quit loop. Start narrow (home training), widen once the audience trusts the brand.

## 3. BRAND VALUES

These are non-negotiable. Every piece of content must respect all six.

1. **Systems over motivation.** Feelings come and go; systems stay. We never sell hype — we hand over tools.
2. **Action over talk.** Every post ends with something to *do*, not just something to *feel*.
3. **Honesty over hype.** No fake grindset, no fake lifestyle, no quick-result lies. Progress is slow and we say so.
4. **Small over perfect.** Two minutes done beats two hours planned. Shrink the start.
5. **Recover, don't restart.** Missing a day is normal. Missing twice is the problem. No guilt, no shame — just get back on.
6. **Quiet strength.** Calm confidence, not shouting. We don't need to look tough; we need to be consistent. Never "alpha," never toxic.

**Values in motion (new in v2):** the values also decide how things *move*.
- *Quiet strength* → things land hard and then stop. No wobble, no bounce, no shouting with effects.
- *Systems over motivation* → system elements (installers, progress bars) move linearly and reliably; "motivation" elements stall, flicker and fail.
- *Honesty* → real footage, real timings, nothing faked to look like proof.

## 4. PERSONALITY & VOICE

**If GainsDom were a person:** A calm, sharp older brother who's been through it. Tells you the truth, then gives you a plan. Slightly dark sense of humor. Slightly mysterious. Never preachy.

**Tone:** Confident · Direct · Psychologically sharp · Darkly funny · Calm · Intelligent

### Voice rules
- Short sentences. Punchy. Lots of full stops.
- Speak to "you." Never lecture "people."
- Name the real psychological reason, not the surface excuse.
- One idea per piece. Cut everything else.
- Use system/engine language naturally — don't overdo it.

### Do / Don't

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| "You're not lazy. You're waiting." | "RISE AND GRIND KINGS 👑🔥" |
| "Do 2 minutes. Only 2." | "Just believe in yourself!" |
| "Missed yesterday? Don't miss today." | "No excuses, losers quit." |
| "Motivation is weather. Discipline is an engine." | Empty quotes with no takeaway |
| Calm, controlled intensity | Shouting, insulting, shaming |
| Honest about how slow progress is | "Get shredded in 7 days" |

### Word bank (use naturally)
Engine · Ignition · Protocol · System · Operator · Log · Mission · Signal · Reps · Run · Install · Override · Boot · Error · Loading · Data · Default · Loop

### Recurring phrases
- "Stop waiting. Start small. Never miss twice."
- "Systems, not motivation."
- "Motivation is weather. Discipline is an engine."
- "One miss is data. Two is a new habit."
- "Start smaller than your excuse."
- "Operator, your move."

### On-screen copy rules (new in v2)
- **Headlines:** max 6 words per screen, ideally 2–3. One line per thought.
- **System text (mono):** can be longer and funnier — it's the "small print" where the dark humor lives. Example: `// still waiting for you to feel ready.`
- **Mono text is lowercase after `//`**, UPPERCASE for labels (`STATUS`, `DAY 014`, `PROTOCOL 01`).
- **Never** emoji in the video itself. Emojis only in captions, max 1.

## 5. VISUAL IDENTITY

**The feeling:** A movie trailer mixed with a computer system booting up. Dark, cinematic, premium — never cheesy or generic.

**References (for the feeling, never to copy):**
- *Title sequences by Kyle Cooper / Fincher films* → restrained, purposeful glitch and type.
- *Linear / Apple product films* → pixel-perfect UI, calm exact timing, nothing extra.
- *Swiss poster design* → one giant word, hard left grid, lots of black.
- *Black-and-white sports films* (silhouettes, hard light) → for footage.

### 5.1 Colors

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `bg` Black | `#0A0A0A` | Almost everything |
| `ink` Off-white | `#F2F2F0` | Main text, live UI |
| `card` Dark gray | `#1A1A1A` | UI windows, panels |
| `bar` Title-bar gray | `#222220` | Window title bars |
| `line` Hairline | `#3A3A3A` | Borders, tracks, brackets |
| `dead` Dead gray | `#5E5E5B` | Stalled/failed things (the "motivation" bar) |
| `dim` Secondary | `#8C8C88` | Labels, mono secondary text |
| **`ignition` Orange** | **`#FF4A1C`** | ONE key word or the action — max 5–10% of the screen |

**The accent rule:** Orange means "this matters / take action." Never decorate with it. **One orange element per screen, maximum.** When a new orange element appears, the previous one turns back to off-white.

**Orange glow:** orange is the only color allowed to glow (as if lit from inside, like a CRT). Glow = the same orange at ~50% opacity, blur 30–40 px. Never glow white text.

**Footage color:** always black & white (see 11.3). Orange is never added to footage, except as a UI element over it.

### 5.2 Typography

Three fonts. Never mix in others.

| Role | Font | Weight | Case | Tracking |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Headlines | **Anton** | 400 | ALL CAPS | 0 |
| System / HUD / labels | **JetBrains Mono** | 500 (labels) / 700 (emphasis) | per 4. | +4% |
| Carousel body | **Inter Tight** | 700 | Sentence case | −1% |

All three are free under the SIL Open Font License (OK for commercial use).

**Type scale for 1080×1920 video (px):**

| Level | Size | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | 420–560 | One word or number filling the width (`99%`, `NOW.`) |
| H1 | 220–320 | 1–2 word headline lines (`YOU'RE` / `NOT LAZY.`) |
| H2 | 110–160 | Inside windows (`NOT RESPONDING.`, `DISCIPLINE.EXE`) |
| Mono L | 34–40 | Typed system lines, checklists |
| Mono M | 26–30 | Labels, `// comments` |
| Mono S | 22–24 | HUD only |

**Type rules:**
- **Fit to width:** headline lines are sized so the longest line fills the text column exactly (844 px, see 5.3). Both lines of a two-line headline use the *same* size.
- **Line height:** 0.93–0.96 × font size for Anton. Tight, like a poster.
- **Left aligned, always** (v2 decision: one alignment for the whole brand; it reads like a terminal and a Swiss poster at once).
- **Punctuation counts:** a full stop at the end of a headline makes it feel final and calm. Use it.
- The accent word goes orange; its full stop stays off-white.

### 5.3 Layout & grid

- **Canvas:** 1080×1920 (9:16).
- **Text column:** left margin **96 px**, text never past **x = 940** (keeps away from the right-side app buttons).
- **Safe band:** key content between **y = 220 and y = 1500**. The bottom ~20% (below 1536) is covered by captions, usernames and buttons; the top ~110 px by the app tabs.
- **Vertical rhythm:** 8 px base grid. Spacing between related things: 24/32/48. Between groups: 96+.
- **Hero position:** the most important element sits slightly above center (y ≈ 700–1100). That's where the eye lands on a phone.
- **Negative space is part of the design.** One idea per screen means most of the screen is black.

### 5.4 The HUD (on every video)

Small, subtle, screen-fixed (it does not shake or zoom with the content):

| Element | Position | Spec |
|---|---|---|
| `GAINSDOM/OS` | top-left, x 96, baseline y 168 | Mono S 24, 700, `ink` |
| `STATUS  <state>` | top-right, right edge x 984, baseline y 168 | Mono S 22, `dim`; the state changes with the story (`WAITING` → `NOT RESPONDING` (orange) → `INSTALLING` → `READY` → `RUNNING`) |
| `// PROTOCOL 01 — <NAME>` | bottom-left, x 96, baseline y 1492 | Mono S 22, `dim` |
| Corner brackets | 56 px from each edge (y 96 and 1824) | 2 px `line`, 44 px arms |

The live **STATUS** is a storytelling device: it tells the viewer what the "system" thinks is happening.

## 6. TASTE RULES (new in v2)

These separate "a template" from "a studio."

1. **One hero per moment.** At any instant, one thing is clearly the most important. Everything else is smaller, grayer or gone.
2. **Three options, pick one.** For every hook, every metaphor, every transition: think of three, choose the strongest, cut the other two completely.
3. **Remove until it breaks.** If removing an element changes nothing, remove it.
4. **Everything has a reason.** Every glitch, shake, cut and sound must be motivated by the story (a crash, an impact, a decision). If you can't say why it's there, it goes.
5. **Contrast creates meaning.** The stalled motivation bar *flickers and dies*; the discipline installer *runs steadily*. Design opposites on purpose.
6. **The template test:** if a frame could be mistaken for a Canva preset, a stock template or a generic AI video, redesign it.
7. **The squint test:** squint at the frame. You should still see one clear shape and read the hero word.
8. **The phone test:** watch the export on a real phone, at arm's length, sound off. If a word can't be read in the time it's on screen, it's too small or too fast.

## 7. MOTION SYSTEM (new in v2)

Personality: **controlled power.** Things arrive fast, stop dead, and hold calmly. The camera breathes, never sits still.

### 7.1 Frame rate & timing
- Edit at **60 fps** for motion graphics (30 fps is acceptable for footage-only edits).
- Work on a **beat grid** (see 12.2). Every slam and cut lands on a beat or half-beat.
- **Reading time:** an on-screen line stays at least `0.4 s + 0.3 s per word` (2 words ≈ 1.0 s). Hooks can be faster because they're felt, not read.
- **No dead holds:** nothing stays completely still for more than **1.5 s**. During holds, keep life in it: a slow push-in, a blinking cursor, a ticking counter, a light sweep.

### 7.2 Easing curves (use these names everywhere)

| Name | Curve (CSS / AE equivalent) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Slam** | scale 114% → 100% in 6 frames, `cubic-bezier(.16,1,.3,1)` (ease-out quint) | Headlines arriving. Hard cut in — no fade. |
| **Snap** | `cubic-bezier(.22,1,.36,1)`, 8–12 frames | Windows, panels, UI appearing |
| **Roll** | `cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)`, 6 frames | Slot-machine word swaps |
| **Glide** | `cubic-bezier(.65,0,.35,1)`, 0.3–0.4 s | Shapes morphing into other shapes |
| **Linear** | linear | Anything that represents *the system working* (progress bars, timers) |
| **Stall** | fast ease-out to ~86%, then crawl, then freeze | Anything that represents *motivation* |

**Banned:** bounce, elastic, overshoot wobble, spin, ease-in-out on entrances (looks like a template), any default "pop" preset.

### 7.3 Signature moves
- **Slam-in:** hard cut in at 114% → 100% (6 frames) + screen shake + sub boom.
- **Screen shake:** offset that decays exponentially in ~0.3 s. Amplitude 4–8 px for normal hits, 12–18 px for the single biggest hit of the video. Only on slams. The HUD never shakes.
- **Typing:** mono text types at 38–46 characters/second (60 for quick labels), with a solid block cursor while typing and a **blinking** cursor (2.4 blinks/s) when idle.
- **Camera drift:** the whole picture slowly scales 100% → 103% across the video, plus a ±2 px wander. Never perfectly still.
- **Push-in on holds:** +4–6% scale over the hold, ease-in-out.
- **Motion blur:** every fast move (slams, rolls, morphs, cursor moves) gets real motion blur (180° shutter). No motion blur on static text.

### 7.4 Glitch rules
- Max **3 glitches per video**, each **2–3 frames**.
- Only at story moments: a crash, an "uninstall", the final cut.
- Recipe: horizontal slice displacement + vertical jump + a faint orange ghost offset + a few white/orange noise blocks. Black & white + orange only — no rainbow RGB.
- Always paired with a short glitch sound.

## 8. TRANSITIONS (new in v2)

**Every transition is motivated by the story.** No transition is chosen "because it looks cool."

### Allowed toolkit

| Name | What happens | Use it when |
|---|---|---|
| **Beat cut** | Hard cut exactly on the beat | Default. Most cuts are this. |
| **Black beat** | Cut to pure black for 4–8 frames, then hard cut in | Before a truth line; after a crash |
| **Slot roll** | The old word rolls up out of a masked line, the new word rolls in (6 frames, motion-blurred) | Correcting/replacing a word (`NOT LAZY.` → `WAITING.`) |
| **Strike morph** | A line strikes through a word, then that line becomes the next element (a progress track, a divider) | Turning a problem into a system |
| **Window unfold** | A UI window expands out of a line or point | Opening a tool/protocol |
| **Match cut** | A shape in shot A becomes a shape in shot B (a round clock → a round button; a bar → a horizon) | Linking two ideas |
| **Glitch cut** | 2–3-frame glitch, then the new scene | Crash / uninstall / final cut (counts toward the 3-glitch limit) |
| **CRT collapse** | The frame squashes to a horizontal line, then to a point, which becomes the cursor | Ending a section into silence or the sign-off |
| **Silence cut** | Music drops out completely for ~0.5 s before the key line | Once per video, before the ACTION |

### Banned
Crossfades between unrelated shots, slides, push/wipe presets, star wipes, zoom-blur presets, spinning, page turns, light-leak overlays, "glitch" packs used on every cut.

## 10. TEXTURE & FINISH (new values in v2)

Applied once, on top of everything, in this order:

1. **Scan lines:** 1 px dark line every 4 px, 16% opacity (invisible on black, adds texture on light areas).
2. **Roll band:** a very soft lighter band (2–3% opacity) slowly rolling down the screen, like an old monitor.
3. **Vignette:** radial, 0% in the center → 55% black at the corners.
4. **Film grain:** 10–20% (overlay), changing every frame at 30 fps. Slightly more on footage, less on pure type.
5. **Orange glow** only on orange elements (5.1).

**Never:** lens flares, light leaks, chromatic aberration on everything, fake film burns, dust packs.

## 11. FOOTAGE & B-ROLL STANDARD (rewritten in v2)

Footage makes the brand feel real; the OS elements make it feel designed. The best GainsDom frames combine both: **real B&W footage + one OS element + one headline.**

### 11.1 Sources (licensing — non-negotiable)

Use only footage you are allowed to use commercially:

1. **Your own footage** (best: 100% yours, and it looks authentic).
2. **Pexels** and **Pixabay** (free for commercial use, no attribution needed).
3. **Mixkit** and **Coverr** (free licenses for video projects).
4. Paid stock (Artgrid, Storyblocks, Envato) if there's a budget — the most cinematic option.

**Never:** clips from movies, series, anime or other creators; footage downloaded from **Pinterest**, TikTok, Instagram or YouTube. Most pins and posts are reposted work owned by someone else, and a single copyright claim can mute or remove a video right when it's taking off. **Pinterest is great as a mood board** — collect pins to agree on a *look*, then recreate that look with licensed footage.

**Keep a license log** (one line per clip): file name · source · original URL · license · date downloaded.

### 11.2 Choosing clips
- **Faceless:** hands, feet, shadows, silhouettes, backs, objects. No recognizable faces.
- **Low-key light:** dark backgrounds, one light source, hard shadows. Avoid bright, flat, "stock" lighting.
- **One subject, lots of empty space** (room for the headline).
- **Motion with direction:** a hand moving, sand falling, a runner moving away. Static shots only if something inside moves (a clock hand, rain on glass).
- **Must match the words exactly** — never random filler.
- Prefer **4K/1080p** and **vertical** originals. Horizontal footage is fine inside a Viewport window or cropped carefully.

### 11.3 The grade (one look for every clip)

Black & white, high contrast, slightly darkened, crushed blacks, soft highlights.

- In CapCut / Premiere / Resolve: Saturation 0 · Contrast +25 · Exposure −0.3 to −0.6 · Blacks −30 · Highlights −15 · Grain 15%.
- In ffmpeg (exact recipe used for the brand):
  ```
  hue=s=0, eq=contrast=1.28:brightness=-0.06:gamma=0.92, curves=all='0/0 0.12/0.03 0.5/0.47 0.9/0.86 1/0.94'
  ```
- When text sits over footage, darken that area further (a black gradient at 50–70%), so the type is always readable.

### 11.4 Editing footage
- **Shot length:** 0.6–1.5 s. Faster in the hook and the action, slightly longer for mood.
- **Cut on action** and on the beat.
- **Speed:** mood shots at 50–80% speed (calm, heavy). Action shots at 100%.
- **Two ways to place footage:** full-bleed behind the headline, or **inside a Viewport window** (part of the OS world). Mix both in one video.
- Add a **Reticle tracker** on the subject when the "system" is watching the struggle.

### 11.5 Shot library (what to film or source)
- **Struggle:** phone glow in a dark room, thumb scrolling, snooze button, alarm clock on black, digital clock flipping to 12:00, hourglass running out, rain on a window at night, an unmade bed, an empty workout mat.
- **Action:** push-up hands, tying shoes, wrapping hands, pull-up bar, a night run (back or silhouette), a stopwatch starting, ticking a checklist.
- **Mood:** empty night streets, fog, a lamp in a dark room, a clock face, a city at night.

## 12. SOUND (rewritten in v2)

Sound is half of the feeling. Design it; don't just add a song.

### 12.1 Music
- Dark, cinematic, slow-building: **dark phonk, dark ambient or minimal trap**, **90–100 BPM**.
- Sources: the platform's own library (TikTok / Instagram sounds, which are licensed on-platform), an original track, or properly licensed music. Never a copyrighted song added to the file yourself.
- Structure follows the video: low and tense (hook) → drop on the truth → steady pulse under the tool → **silence** → biggest hit on the action → tail under the sign-off.

### 12.2 The beat grid
- At 96 BPM, one beat = **0.625 s**. Put the first hit at 0.05 s, then every slam/cut on a beat or half-beat after it.
- Plan the beat sheet in seconds **and** frames (at 60 fps, a beat is 37.5 frames).

### 12.3 Sound design kit

| Sound | When | Level (relative) |
|---|---|---|
| Sub boom (40–60 Hz, ~1.5 s tail) | Every slam-in | Loud |
| Hit (punchy transient) | Slam + the biggest moments | Loud |
| Keyboard ticks | Every typed mono line | Quiet |
| UI click | Buttons, check marks | Medium-quiet |
| Glitch burst (≤ 0.1 s) | Each glitch | Medium |
| Whoosh (short, filtered) | Rolls, morphs, fast moves | Quiet |
| Error tone (2 low notes) | Error dialog | Medium |
| Completion chime | Install complete / streak recovered | Medium-quiet |
| CRT-off zap | CRT collapse | Quiet |

### 12.4 Silence
Cut **all** sound for ~0.5 s right before the most important line. Then hit it with the biggest boom of the video.

### 12.5 Loudness
- Final mix **−14 LUFS integrated**, true peak **−1 dBTP**.
- Even though many watch muted, sound-on viewers decide whether to share. Treat sound as equal to picture.

## 13. CONTENT PILLARS & FORMULA

### Pillar 1 — Protocol Drops (teach)
Concise, practical protocols for discipline, fitness, focus and growth. Numbered (Protocol 01, 02, 03…) so the page feels like a growing system. **Goal:** saves.

### Pillar 2 — Meme-to-Mission Bridge (relate → teach)
Start with a relatable or darkly funny situation, then turn it into an actionable lesson. The joke earns attention; the lesson earns the follow. **A meme without a mission is not GainsDom.** **Goal:** shares + follows.

### Pillar 3 — Operator Log (reflect)
Diary-style reflections on progress, setbacks and systems. Moody B-roll + a short, honest line. Numbered by day. **Goal:** connection — people come back for the story.

### Signature formats (used across pillars)
- **Fake system screens:** `MOTIVATION.EXE`, loading bars stuck at 99%, `ERROR: DISCIPLINE NOT INSTALLED`.
- **Carousels:** 5–7 slides. Slide 1 hook → steps → last slide "Save this + follow."

### Mix (starting point)
~40% Protocol · ~40% Meme-to-Mission · ~20% Operator Log. Adjust based on results.

### Brand formula
**Clear enemy + clear promise + consistent identity + repetition over time = brand.**

### Content formula — every piece
**HOOK → TRUTH → TOOL → ACTION**

1. **HOOK** — A relatable pain, a joke or a bold statement. Stops the scroll in under 2 seconds.
2. **TRUTH** — The real psychological reason behind the struggle.
3. **TOOL** — One system, rule or protocol. Concrete and small.
4. **ACTION** — What to do right now. ("Start now." / "Save this." / "Do 2 minutes.")

**If a piece is missing any of the four, it isn't GainsDom.**

### The three "ones"
Every piece has: **one strong idea · one memorable visual metaphor · one practical takeaway.**

## 14. VIDEO STRUCTURE

**Length:** 7–20 seconds at first (15 s is the sweet spot).
**Pacing:** new text or visual every 1–2 seconds. Faster at the start, slightly slower for the lesson. End sharp.

### 14.1 The 15-second template (frame-accurate, 96 BPM, 60 fps)

| Time (s) | Frames | Beat | What happens | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00–0.05 | 0–3 | — | Black + block cursor (matches the last frame: loop) | — |
| 0.05–2.55 | 3–153 | **HOOK** | Strongest line slams in at frame 3 over B&W footage or an OS element. 2–3 fast footage cuts on beats. No logo, no intro. | Boom on frame 3, ticks, tension |
| 2.55–3.18 | 153–191 | Twist | The excuse "crashes" (error / glitch / black beat) | Error tone, glitch, silence |

### 14.2 Hook library

**Callout:** "You're not lazy. You're waiting." · "If you keep quitting, watch this." · "The reason you never stay consistent:" · "You don't have a motivation problem."

### 14.3 Hook craft (new in v2)
- **Motion in frame 1.** The first frame must already be moving or slamming — never a static title or a fade from black.

- **Open loop:** the hook creates a question the TOOL answers.
- **Loop the ending** into the first frame so rewatches feel seamless (rewatches push reach).

## 16. PRODUCTION PIPELINE (new in v2)

The same 6 steps for every video:

1. **Brief** — fill the output format (18). Three hooks, pick one.
2. **Beat sheet** — seconds + frames on the 96 BPM grid, with element, transition and shot for each beat.
3. **Style frames** — make 2–3 finished stills first (the hook, the tool, the action). Fixing a still is cheap; fixing an animation is not. Run the taste tests (6).
4. **Build** — animate from the element templates (9), footage graded (11.3), sound designed on the grid (12).
5. **Screen it** — watch the export on a phone, sound off, then sound on. Step through it frame by frame around every cut. Then score it (17.1). Anything under 8 → fix and export again. **Never post the first export.**
6. **Ship** — export (17.3), cover frame, caption, post, log the result.

**Tools:**
- Free: CapCut (edit + text), Canva (style frames, carousels), DaVinci Resolve (grade).
- Pro: After Effects (element templates, motion blur, precise easing).
- Code (what this brand's hero videos use): a canvas renderer driven by a timeline, so every frame is exact and repeatable.

### 17.1 Scorecard (every line must score 8/10 or more)

| Criterion | What 10/10 looks like |
|---|---|

| Sound | Hits on the frame, silence before the action, −14 LUFS |
| Brand | Looks and sounds like every other GainsDom video |
| Big-brand test | Could sit next to an Apple/Nike vertical ad without embarrassment |

### 17.2 Brand checklist
- [ ] HOOK → TRUTH → TOOL → ACTION all present?
- [ ] Hook stops a scroll in under 2 seconds?
- [ ] Exactly one idea, one metaphor, one takeaway?
- [ ] Takeaway worth saving or sharing?
- [ ] Sounds like a calm, sharp older brother — not a hype page?

### 17.3 Technical checklist & export
- **Format:** MP4, H.264 (High profile), 1080×1920, **30 or 60 fps** (keep the edit's fps), progressive.
- **Bitrate:** 12–20 Mbps video, AAC 320 kbps 48 kHz audio. (A 15 s video ≈ 25–40 MB.)
- **Color:** Rec.709, full black stays black (check for gray "lifted" blacks after export).
- **Loudness:** −14 LUFS, −1 dBTP.
- **Safe zones:** no key text below y 1536 or right of x 940 (check with a safe-zone overlay).
- **First frame:** works as a cover (or set a custom cover: the hook headline frame).
- **Last frame:** black + cursor = matches the first frame for a clean loop.
- **No banding** in dark gradients (add grain before export if needed).
