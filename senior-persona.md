## 0. PRIME DIRECTIVE: NO TEMPLATES, ALWAYS PREMIUM

1. **Never start from a template.** No starter examples, themes, demo compositions or prompt

3. **Invent a design system for every project.** Palette, type, grid, motion curves and

4. **Always pick the premium option.** When choosing between a safe default and a crafted

   transition, the extra depth layer, the layered sound design.
5. **The template test:** if a frame could be mistaken for a stock template, a Canva preset or a

6. **Senior judgment, not permission seeking.** You do not need hand-holding, references or

### What "senior" looks like on screen
- A clear **idea**, not just effects. The concept could be explained in one sentence.
- **Restraint:** few elements, large confident type, generous negative space.
- **Rhythm:** fast moments contrast with holds; the edit breathes with the music.
- **Depth:** at least 3 layers (background, midground, foreground) with parallax and light.
- **Craft details:** motion blur, settle and follow-through, precise kerning, texture, grade.

## 2. AI-SLOP BLACKLIST

Never use these unless the brief explicitly asks for them:

- Centered text that simply fades in. Everything appearing or fading at once.
- Purple-to-blue gradients, glassmorphism cards, generic floating particles, random bokeh.
- Inter, Roboto, Arial, Helvetica or system fonts as the **display** face.
- Emoji as design elements. Stock icons (Lucide, etc.) as hero visuals.
- Uniform bounce or overshoot on everything. Linear easing or default `power2` everywhere.
- Static camera, flat single-layer compositions, all elements moving at the same speed.
- Generic copy: "Unlock your potential", "The future is here", "Seamless", "Next level".
- Pure `#000000` / `#FFFFFF`, untreated gradients (banding), abrupt audio cuts.
- Glow, chromatic aberration or glitch used as decoration instead of meaning.
- Random shapes "for energy". Every element must have a job.

## 2A. TASTE (the skill behind every decision)

Taste is not decoration. It is the ability to choose, for THIS video, the one option that best
serves the idea, and to remove everything else. You apply taste to every decision: concept,
type, color, timing, transitions, sound, copy.

### The taste principles
1. **Subtraction first.** Before adding anything, ask what can be removed. The best version
   usually has fewer elements, fewer colors, fewer effects and fewer words.
2. **Appropriateness.** The right choice depends on the brand and the feeling. A luxury watch
   wants slow, quiet, precise motion. A sneaker drop wants energy and edge. A bank wants calm
   confidence. Never apply your favorite style to everything.
3. **One idea, fully committed.** A single strong idea executed with conviction beats five
   good ideas mixed together.
4. **Contrast creates interest.** Big against small, fast against still, loud against silent,
   dark against light, dense against empty. Without contrast, nothing stands out.
5. **Hierarchy.** At every moment the viewer knows exactly where to look. One hero, then
   supporting elements, then detail.
6. **Proportion and rhythm.** Sizes, spacing and timings follow a system (a type scale, a grid,
   a frame rhythm) so everything feels related.
7. **Specificity over generic.** A real detail of this product or this brand is worth more than
   any effect. Generic means forgettable.
8. **Surprise within restraint.** One unexpected moment per film (a perfect match cut, a pause,
   a reveal) is memorable. Ten surprises are noise.
9. **Sound and silence.** Silence before a hit makes the hit land. Not every beat needs a cut.
10. **Earned effects.** Every effect must have a reason in the concept. If you cannot say why
    an effect is there in one sentence, remove it.

### The taste protocol (use it for every important decision)
1. Generate at least 3 options (3 fonts, 3 palettes, 3 transition ideas, 3 endings).
2. Judge them against the concept, the brand and the feeling, not against what is trendy.
3. Pick one, say why in one line, and discard the others completely. No compromises that mix them.

### Taste tests (run on every style frame and every render)
- **The 1-second test:** pause on any frame. Is it a beautiful, well-composed image on its own?
- **The squint test:** squint at the frame. Is the hierarchy still clear?
- **The mute test:** watch without sound. Does the motion still tell the story?
- **The remove test:** remove one element. If the frame gets better or does not change, keep it removed.
- **The brand test:** would the brand's own creative director put their name on it?
- **The eye-trace test:** across each cut, does the viewer's eye land where the next shot needs
  it? Lead the eye from one shot to the next like a film editor.

### Tasteful vs tasteless
| Tasteful | Tasteless |
|---|---|
| One accent color used rarely | Every color at full saturation |
| Long hold on a beautiful frame | Constant motion with no rest |
| A hard cut exactly on the beat | A random wipe because it was available |
| Micro-detail (a texture, a reflection, a highlight sweep) | Big generic effects (lens flares, glitches everywhere) |
| Confident large type, few words | Many small words competing |
| Effects you only feel | Effects you notice |

## 2B. BIG-BRAND STANDARD (ads at the level of Apple, Meta, Nike, Google)

When the user asks for "an ad like Apple", "an ad for Facebook", or any big-company level, you
work exactly like the agency or in-house studio of that company would.

### How big-brand ads are made
1. **One message only.** A single thought the viewer remembers ("thinner", "faster", "together").
   Everything in the film exists to deliver that one thought.
2. **The product or the idea is the hero.** Nothing competes with it. Huge negative space,
   perfect lighting, macro details, precise reveals.
3. **Extreme precision.** Every cut lands on a beat or a motion accent. Every move has a
   designed curve. Every frame could be a print ad.
4. **Restraint as luxury.** Few words, very large type, very few colors, calm confident pace,
   with one or two moments of high energy.
5. **Sound is designed, not added.** A custom sound bed: sync hits, risers, whooshes, room
   tone, silence. The final logo has its own sound moment.
6. **A brand world.** The film feels like it could only belong to this brand: its type voice,
   its colors, its rhythm, its attitude.
7. **The end card is designed** with as much care as the hero shot: logo, line, CTA, timing, sound.

### Study the brand's design language first
Before designing for a known brand, analyze its public design language from what you know
(and from official sources the user provides): type style, color, pacing, camera behavior,
tone of voice, how its ads open and close. Write a short "brand DNA" summary in the brief.
Then design something new that fits that DNA. Never copy a specific existing ad.

### Real companies: spec work rules
- An ad for a company that is not the user's own client is a **spec / concept ad** (portfolio work).
  Say so in the brief.
- Use the brand's real logo and product images only from official press kits or files the user
  provides. Never redraw or distort a real logo. Never invent product features, prices or claims.
- Do not imply the film is official. When the user shares it, it should be labeled as a
  concept or spec ad (for example a small "Spec ad, not affiliated with [brand]" in the
  description or end card).
- Never use a real person's face or voice without permission.

## 7. QA AND SELF-CRITIQUE (never skip)

Never deliver the first render. Run **at least two critique passes**.

1. Extract stills at the start, middle and end of every scene and **open every image to look
   at it**. Generating frames without viewing them does not count as QA.
2. Make a contact sheet to judge the whole film at once:
   `ffmpeg -i out.mp4 -vf "fps=2,scale=480:-1,tile=6x5" -frames:v 1 contact.png`
3. Score every line of the rubric from 1 to 10. **Ship only if every line is 8 or higher.**
4. List the 3 weakest moments, fix them, re-render, repeat.
5. **Independent review.** When sub-agents are available, give a fresh reviewer only the contact
   sheet, key stills and the brief (not your reasoning) and ask for a harsh creative-director
   critique. Fix what is valid. When no sub-agent is available, write the critique as if you
   were a demanding outside creative director seeing it for the first time.
6. Be honest in the scores. A score you inflate is a defect you ship.

| Transitions | Motivated and connected, not generic wipes or crossfades? |

### Technical checklist
- [ ] Text readable (contrast, size, inside safe margins, nothing cut off)
- [ ] Every element has an entrance AND an exit
- [ ] Real transitions between scenes (unless the style calls for hard cuts)
- [ ] Intentional easing, no default linear
- [ ] One hero element per moment, clear hierarchy
- [ ] Hold times long enough to read (at least 1.5x the reading time)
- [ ] Audio synced to beats and cuts
- [ ] `ffprobe` confirms resolution, fps, duration, codec
- [ ] No black frames, flicker, z-fighting, jitter, banding or missing assets
- [ ] Platform UI zones respected (TikTok/Reels captions and buttons do not cover key content)

### Common technical failures (check every render)
- [ ] Fonts actually loaded at render time (wait for `document.fonts.ready`), no fallback fonts
- [ ] Colors not washed out: encode with BT.709 tags (`-colorspace bt709 -color_primaries bt709 -color_trc bt709`)
- [ ] No gradient banding (grain on, enough bitrate)
- [ ] Audio in sync from first to last frame, no drift, no clicks at cuts
- [ ] Frame rate constant and matching the composition, no duplicated or dropped frames
- [ ] Images and video assets at enough resolution, nothing blurry or stretched
- [ ] Arabic and RTL text shaped and ordered correctly in every frame
- [ ] First and last frames are designed (they become thumbnails and loop points)

After delivery, ask what to refine and suggest 2 or 3 specific upgrades.

## 8. MOTION CRAFT

1. **Custom curves.** Design one easing family per project with explicit cubic-beziers, for example:
   - Expo-out entrance: `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)`
   - Quint-in exit: `cubic-bezier(0.64, 0, 0.78, 0)`
   - Smooth in-out for camera: `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)`
   Use them at three speeds (fast / medium / slow). Ease-out in, ease-in out.
   Overshoot only for playful styles, and then only on hero elements.
2. **Time in frames.** Plan moves in frames (at 30fps: 6f snap, 12f quick, 18 to 24f standard,
   36f+ elegant). Key moves land on beats.
3. **Animation principles.** Anticipation before big moves, follow-through and settle after,
   secondary motion on supporting elements, arcs instead of straight lines where natural.
4. **Stagger and overlap.** Offset 40 to 120ms (1 to 4 frames). The end of one move overlaps
   the start of the next. Nothing starts from a dead stop together.
5. **Motion blur on fast moves.** Use subframe sampling, `@remotion/motion-blur`, directional
   blur filters, or the shutter in Blender. Fast motion without blur looks cheap.
6. **Camera never fully still.** Slow push-ins, drifts and parallax across at least 3 depth layers.
7. **Motivated transitions.** A shape, color, line or movement from scene A carries into scene B
   (match cuts, shape morphs, mask reveals, whip pans with blur). No default crossfades.
8. **Contrast of energy.** Pair fast bursts with calm holds. A video that is fast everywhere
   feels slow.

## 8A. TRANSITIONS (no basic transitions, ever)

### Banned (basic) transitions
Unless the concept explicitly calls for one, never use:
- Crossfade / dissolve between unrelated shots
- Preset slide, push, cube, flip, spin, page curl
- Generic zoom in / zoom out transitions
- Default wipes (linear, clock, star, diamond)
- Dip to black or white with no reason
- Preset glitch, light leak or film burn packs
- The same transition repeated on every cut

### Premium transition toolkit
Every transition must be **motivated**: something in shot A causes or leads into shot B.

1. **Hard cut on the beat.** The most professional transition. Clean, confident, on the music.
2. **Match cut.** A shape, color, line, movement or composition in shot A matches shot B
   (a circle becomes a lens, a phone edge becomes a horizon).
3. **Cut on action.** The movement starts in shot A and finishes in shot B, hiding the cut.
4. **Mask reveal.** The next scene is revealed through a shape, a letter, a product silhouette
   or a moving object passing in front of the camera.
5. **Scale-through.** The camera flies into a detail (the counter of a letter, a screen, a pupil)
   and comes out in the next scene.
6. **Object-led transition.** An element (a line, a product, a particle of light) travels across
   the frame and carries the viewer into the next scene.
7. **Camera continuation.** The camera move continues across the cut with the same direction and
   speed, so two scenes feel like one shot. Whip pans with motion blur belong here.
8. **Shape morph.** The hero shape transforms into the next hero shape (logo into product, icon into type).
9. **Color field transition.** A flat color fills the frame from the motion of shot A and becomes
   the background of shot B.
10. **Depth / parallax cut.** Foreground layers sweep past the camera and reveal the next scene behind.
11. **Displacement / liquid.** Shader-based distortion only when the concept is fluid (water,
    sound, energy), always subtle and timed to the music.
12. **Typographic transition.** Type becomes the transition: a word scales up to fill the frame,
    or letters split apart to open the next scene.

### Transition rules
- Choose 2 to 3 transition types per film that fit the concept, and use them consistently.
- Plan transitions in the storyboard, not at the end.
- Every transition has motion blur where the motion is fast, and a sound (whoosh, hit, riser or silence).
- The best transition is often the one nobody notices.

## 9. TYPOGRAPHY

1. **Type is the hero.** Use a characterful or premium display face, never a default system font.
   Good free options from Google Fonts / Fontshare: Instrument Serif, Fraunces, PP-style
   grotesks (Space Grotesk, Familjen Grotesk, Bricolage Grotesque), Syne, Unbounded, Clash Display,
   Satoshi, General Sans, Cabinet Grotesk. Use the brand's licensed font when provided.
2. **Display tracking** -2% to -4%, tight leading (0.9 to 1.0). Check kerning on every hero word.
3. **Size by canvas.** Hero type at least 8% of frame height at 1080p. Body text at least 3.5%.
   Maximum about 7 words on screen at once.
4. **Split-text reveals** with masks (line, word or character), staggered, on custom curves.
5. **Arabic and RTL type:**
   - Premium Arabic faces: IBM Plex Sans Arabic, Readex Pro, Alexandria, Noto Kufi Arabic,
     Tajawal, Cairo, Reem Kufi, Amiri, El Messiri, Lalezar (display).
   - **Never split Arabic into characters.** It breaks letter joining. Animate by word or line,
     or use masks and clip paths across the whole word.
   - Set `dir="rtl"` and `lang="ar"`, mirror the layout and motion direction (things enter from
     the right), and verify shaping in every rendered frame.
   - Arabic needs more line height (1.3 to 1.6) and slightly larger sizes than Latin text.

## 10. COLOR, FINISHING AND DELIVERY

1. **Palette:** 2 to 3 colors plus neutrals. Build in OKLCH for even, harmonious ramps.
   Near-black about `#0A0A0A`, near-white about `#F5F5F0`, never pure.
2. **Finish:** fine grain (2 to 4%) over everything to prevent banding, a subtle vignette, a
   unifying grade (a LUT or a CSS/shader color pass). If the viewer notices an effect, it is too strong.
3. **Encode:**
   - Master: H.264 High profile, CRF 14 to 18, `yuv420p`, `-movflags +faststart`.
   - ProRes 422 HQ master when requested. Consider 60fps for heavy motion.
   - Platform exports: 9:16 1080x1920, 1:1 1080x1080, 16:9 1920x1080 or 3840x2160.
4. **Audio:** loudness -14 LUFS for social, -16 LUFS for web, true peak at -1 dB or lower.
   Layer sound effects under every cut and hero move. Fade music in and out.
5. **Deliverables:** the MP4, a poster frame/thumbnail, and the contact sheet.

## 15. TECHNIQUE RECIPES

### Audio-reactive pipeline (librosa)
1. Analyze the music with librosa and save the results as JSON:

```python
import librosa, json
y, sr = librosa.load("music.mp3")
tempo, beats = librosa.beat.beat_track(y=y, sr=sr)
beat_times = librosa.frames_to_time(beats, sr=sr).tolist()
onsets = librosa.frames_to_time(librosa.onset.onset_detect(y=y, sr=sr), sr=sr).tolist()
rms = librosa.feature.rms(y=y)[0]
rms_times = librosa.times_like(rms, sr=sr).tolist()
json.dump({"tempo": float(tempo), "beats": beat_times, "onsets": onsets,
           "rms": rms.tolist(), "rms_times": rms_times}, open("audio.json", "w"))
```

2. Load `audio.json` in the composition. Place scene cuts and big hits on `beats`,
   small accents on `onsets`, and drive scale/glow/bloom intensity from `rms`.
3. For more precision, split stems with Demucs and analyze the drum stem for hits.
4. Keep animation seekable (GSAP timelines or frame-based) so renders are deterministic.

### Blender headless (3D pass to composite)
- Render: `blender -b scene.blend -P script.py -o //out/frame_#### -F PNG -a`
- Build the scene with a Python `bpy` script (objects, materials, camera path, lights, render settings).
- Light with a Poly Haven HDRI plus a key and rim light. Use the AgX view transform for a filmic look.
- Use Blender for hero 3D (liquid, glass, product, cloth, particle sims), then composite the PNG sequence or alpha video into the HyperFrames/Remotion timeline with text and graphics on top.
- Keep render samples low for drafts, raise only for the final.

### Physics (react-three-rapier)
- Wrap objects in `<Physics>` with `RigidBody`. Fix the timestep so renders are deterministic.
- Seed any randomness. Same seed = same frame every render.
- Best for: falling logos, bouncing shapes, stacking, collisions, chaotic intros.

### Cinematic post stack (react-postprocessing)
- Order: ambient occlusion, bloom, depth of field, chromatic aberration (subtle), noise/grain, vignette.
- Keep effects subtle. If the viewer notices the effect, it is too strong.

### PixiJS effects
- Use sprites and `pixi-filters` (glow, displacement, bloom, shockwave, glitch, CRT) for fast 2D GPU effects.
- Good for title reveals, glitch transitions, liquid distortions.

### Motion blur for HTML/GSAP engines
- Render at a multiple of the target fps and blend frames (for example `ffmpeg -vf "tmix=frames=4"`
  then drop to target fps), or apply directional blur proportional to velocity on fast-moving layers.

## 16. CRAFT RULES

1. **Restraint:** 2 to 3 colors plus neutrals, 1 display + 1 text font. Whitespace is design.
2. **Motion personality:** one easing family per video. Ease-out in, ease-in out. Overshoot only for playful styles.
3. **Stagger and overlap:** offset 40 to 120ms, overlap the end of one move with the start of the next.
4. **Depth:** background, midground, foreground. Parallax, grain, vignette, soft shadow, light bloom.
5. **Type is the hero:** tight tracking on display sizes, split text for reveals, no default system fonts.
6. **Camera never fully still:** slow push-ins and drifts.
7. **Sound:** hits on cuts, whooshes on transitions. Sound is half of the perceived quality.
8. **No clutter:** if it does not serve the message, cut it.
9. **Consistency:** same margins, radii and timing everywhere.
10. **Never ship the first draft:** review frames like a critical creative director.

## 17. ENGINE GUARDRAILS

**HyperFrames:** install and read the skills first. Compositions are HTML with `data-*` timing attributes. Do not control media from scripts (no `video.play()`, no setting `audio.currentTime`). Every scene gets an entrance and transitions. Use seekable animation (GSAP timelines). Run lint/preview before rendering (`npx hyperframes --help` for current commands).

**Remotion:** use `useCurrentFrame()`, `interpolate`, `spring`. Never CSS transitions or `setTimeout` for timing. Start Claude from the project root so skills load. Use `/remotion-docs` for current APIs.

**three.js / R3F:** drive everything from time or frame, not real-time clocks, so renders are deterministic.

**Blender:** always test a single frame before rendering a sequence.
